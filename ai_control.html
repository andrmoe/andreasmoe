The model and its limits

For a common reference setting, the best-protocol safety score follows:

logit(S) = α + p × t + βM × ln(M / 4 min) − βA × ln(A / 4 min)

The attacker and monitor coefficients are estimated independently. Each study can have different capability responses. The monitor capability input is always the trusted model. Untrusted-helper identity and capability are not modeled, and helper configurations are pooled within each bootstrapping architecture. Their reported outcomes remain evidence, with extra residual uncertainty for the pooled variation. The observation model also includes benchmark, paired evaluation, metric, protocol, attack-elicitation and resource effects. Published protocols may fall short of the latent best protocol; an ordinary attack may overstate robustness against a more thoroughly elicited attacker.

For each candidate i published at time ti, its inferred method effectiveness is frozen at that date, with an uncertain shortfall di below the latent method frontier. The graph selects the best available candidate separately in each draw:

ln(CP(t) / 4 min) = [α + maxi published by t(p × ti − di) + βM × ln(M(t) / 4 min) − logit(P)] / βA

The weakest-monitor view inverts the same posterior for a chosen attacker capability A(t). Lower required monitor capability means better control:

ln(MP(t) / 4 min) = [logit(P) − α − maxi published by t(p × ti − di) + βA × ln(A(t) / 4 min)] / βM

Intervals retain 90% posterior coverage. Values below one minute are clipped for display and marked by downward arrows. This convention does not impose a lower bound on the fitted posterior or define a METR benchmark minimum.

The question about relative growth is therefore:

control gains ground when observed-envelope method gain per year + βM × gM > βA × gfrontier

The frontier growth reference comes from METR’s published v1.1 trend, with additional trend uncertainty. The blue dots retain this page’s existing METR and ECI inputs; the ECI conversion has not been refitted.

  • Recall, per-episode prevention and deployment safety are different quantities. Their uncertain relationship is a transport assumption, not an exact conversion.
  • The line sets study effects to their reference values and retains each candidate's inferred shortfall. It estimates a best-available envelope, not a sequence of directly demonstrated records. Leadership probabilities are conditional on this normalization.
  • A smooth, approximately log-linear trend is assumed over this short period. Difficulty drift and genuine protocol progress are not separately identified by the papers.
  • Safety curves are assumed to shift locally with capability. Uncovered channels, new attack classes or a persistent failure floor could invalidate the extrapolation, especially at 95% or 99%.
  • Usefulness, auditing, latency and inference budgets vary. The uncertainty adjustments do not amount to a validated comparison at identical cost. Candidate rankings also depend on how finely protocol variants are distinguished.
  • Holding a hypothetical one-hour monitor fixed projects it back into years when it may not have existed. Growing at the frontier rate means a fixed relative capability gap; it does not mean the frontier itself is trusted.

Trusted AI-control growth: exploratory evidence synthesis

This analysis asks whether the best control capability available with an honest model is increasing faster than frontier capability. It implements a conditional Bayesian model, rather than interpreting heterogeneous paper results as measurements on a common scale.

The reference fit favors slower growth with a fixed trusted model. Its probability of control growing faster is 16%, ranging from 4–51% across the twelve tested assumption sets. If trusted capability grows half as fast as frontier capability, the reference estimate is 60% (range 30–88%). If it maintains a fixed capability ratio to the frontier, the estimate is 93% (range 80–99%). These ranges are sensitivity analyses, not confidence intervals or bounds covering every possible model.

These are model-conditioned probabilities, not calibrated odds that humanity can control future AI. The trend is weakly identified across changing benchmarks. A single preferred percentage conceals a substantial dependence on assumptions. The model estimates a latent best published-method frontier, with uncertain shortfalls for individual evaluated protocols; it cannot establish the optimal control possible in principle.

Observation model

The trusted model is the only monitor-capability input. The raw evidence ledger preserves study provenance, but model_observations removes helper-specific conditions and pools helper configurations within architectural bootstrap variants before building the design or candidate list. No untrusted helper identity or capability is an explanatory variable or a ranking dimension. All 650 outcomes remain. Pooled bootstrapping-study observations receive an additional residual standard deviation of 1.5 log-odds to acknowledge unexplained variation; this scale is a modeling assumption. Helper identities changing cannot change the fit inputs or candidate list, as checked by a regression test.

Let A be attacker horizon, M trusted monitor horizon, and t years since 12 December 2023. A common reference best-protocol score is:

logit(S*) = alpha + p*t + beta_M*ln(M/4 min) - beta_A*ln(A/4 min)

The response coefficients are estimated independently. The observed logit score additionally includes:

  • Shared study offsets, paired evaluation-group offsets (Normal(0, 1)), and study-specific attacker/monitor response deviations.
  • Shared metric offsets for recall and per-episode prevention relative to safety.
  • Nonnegative protocol shortfalls below the latent best protocol.
  • Ordinary-attack optimism, separately elicited attack penalties, and tested-model effects where applicable. No helper-specific condition effects are fitted.
  • An uncertain resource advantage for the lower-usefulness deferral result.
  • Systematic benchmark-difficulty drift, which is not separately identified from genuine progress.

The relationship between recall and deployment safety is not an exact logit offset. Using an uncertain offset is an explicit working approximation. It allows incomplete observations to inform the synthesis without pretending their metrics are interchangeable. Its adequacy is a major source of structural uncertainty.

For LinuxArena, if h is successful undetected sabotage and c is detected attacks, safety is c/(c+h), with c <= 1-h. Therefore safety <= 1-h. The old page used 1-h as if it measured attacks caught; this model uses the inequality. For EvasionBench's best-of-three probability H3, the union bound gives mean single-run success >= H3/3 without independent attempts. Transport of the resulting mixed-agent bound is intentionally weak.

Unknown horizons use a broad working capability distribution around a centering convention; the center is not a measurement. Horizon errors are propagated approximately into working logit errors using a reference response of 0.6. This is not a full errors-in-variables treatment. Cross-domain capability transfer, source/model dependence beyond the included effects, and missing cost curves remain unresolved.

Relative growth and the capability curve

At a fixed safety target P, the smooth latent-frontier horizon would be:

ln(C_P / 4 min) = [alpha + p*t + beta_M*ln(M(t)/4 min) - logit(P)] / beta_A

For the plotted best-available envelope, each candidate i has method score pt_i - d_i, where t_i is its publication time and d_i its inferred shortfall. At each date, take the maximum score over available candidates separately in each posterior draw. Replace pt in the capability equation with this maximum. This is not the score of the single modal leader.

The reported historical growth probability uses the difference in the envelope score between the first and last evidence dates, divided by elapsed years. Call this method gain g_P. If trusted capability grows at g_M and frontier capability at g_F:

g_C = (g_P + beta_M*g_M) / beta_A
control grows faster iff g_P + beta_M*g_M > beta_A*g_F

Shared reference response curves make candidate rankings invariant to safety target and monitor size after normalization. Protocol-specific response shapes and resource tradeoffs are not sufficiently identified to claim that this invariance is an empirical fact. Rankings are also sensitive to variant granularity: a family with many independently parameterized variants has more opportunities to supply the maximum. Shared study and evaluation effects reduce evidence duplication but do not eliminate this model-selection issue.

This separates improvements in methods from access to more capable honest models. It does not impose proportional monitor scaling or equal attacker/monitor coefficients. Changing P or the reference monitor size changes the height, not the growth probability, within this local scaling approximation.

METR's published from-2023 doubling time is 128.744 days, corresponding to log growth 1.966/year. Inference uses additional standard deviation 0.35/year, broader than a simple regression sampling error. The former page's capability inputs are retained; this analysis does not re-estimate the ECI conversion or validate that METR horizon is the correct attack-capability scale.

Priors and sensitivity

Reference priors, in logit units:

  • Intercept: Normal(0, 4).
  • Annual method progress p: HalfNormal(1). Nonnegative because previously available techniques can be retained for a fixed reference problem and budget; actual evaluated scores may still deteriorate.
  • Global monitor and attacker responses: independent Normal(0.6, 0.8), each truncated above zero. The common prior is not an equality constraint and is not an empirical LinuxArena slope estimate.
  • Study/metric offsets: Normal(0, 1.5).
  • Study-specific response deviations: Normal(0, 0.35). Actual local responses need not be positive.
  • Systematic benchmark drift: Normal(0, 0.5)/year.
  • Protocol shortfalls: HalfNormal(1); ordinary-attack optimism: HalfNormal(1.5).
  • Elicitation penalties: HalfNormal(2); tested-model effects: Normal(0, 1).
  • Low-usefulness resource advantage: HalfNormal(1.5).

The nine primary fits cross study/metric transport standard deviations {0.75, 1.5, 3} with method-progress prior scales {0.5, 1, 2}. Three further fits allow more systematic difficulty drift, less transferable capability slopes, or jointly weak identification with larger protocol shortfalls. No single row is selected because it gives a preferred conclusion.

An additional stress test subtracts 0.5 or 1 annual log-odds of unmodeled attack advantage from the growth margin. These rates are scenarios, not estimates from the papers.

Missing comparisons do not identify a likelihood without assumptions. In particular, the evidence cannot tell us whether all unobserved future attack classes are covered, whether a persistent safety ceiling exists, or whether development effort and useful-work budgets have stayed comparable. The model cannot demonstrate 95% or 99% robust control merely by shifting a sigmoid.

The published expanded fit uses four chains per assumption set, 1,000 warmup and 1,000 retained iterations per chain; the script defaults to 2,500 each for a longer run. Gaussian nuisance effects are analytically marginalized; constrained coefficients and censored observations use exact Gibbs conditionals for the working Gaussian likelihood. The graph exports 2,000 reproducibly selected reference-posterior draws from the same fit.

data.json → results contains probabilities, intervals, priors, convergence diagnostics, structural sensitivities and reference fitted observations. The assumptions table summarizes prior sensitivity. The complete baseline posterior is generated locally as baseline_draws.npz; the lightweight page data is generated at the repository root.

The independent tests compare the sampler against direct multivariate-Gaussian rejection sampling and check extreme-tail truncation and the growth comparison algebra. Numerical convergence does not validate the scientific assumptions. Reference and sensitivity fits passed split R-hat < 1.05 and effective sample size > 400 for displayed growth coefficients.

Inverse monitor view and display floor

The graph toggle can instead display the weakest sufficient trusted monitor for a selected attacker horizon, held fixed or growing at the frontier rate. Each posterior draw solves:

ln(M_P / 4 min) = [logit(P) - alpha - best_method_score(t) + beta_A*ln(A(t)/4 min)] / beta_M

The same draw-wise best method minimizes required monitor capability; lower is better in this view. Its attacker input defaults to 24 hours at the reference date. The forward view retains its separate trusted-monitor input and growth scenarios. The forward growth-probability headline is hidden in the inverse view to avoid interpreting it as a monitor-requirement probability.

Both views retain 90% intervals. The display clips horizons below one minute and gives control error bars a downward arrow when their lower endpoint crosses that floor. Readouts describe such estimates as below the display limit. This is a plotting convention, not a METR minimum or posterior truncation. No inference data or posterior draws are changed.
