Can internal signals make safety training durable?

The next promising route: change what the model learns, so fewer dangerous behaviors need to be caught after training.

Current judgment: worth a focused durability test. Internal signals can improve training in selected settings, and larger models sometimes benefit more. But the gains can fail under distribution shift or subsequent training. This route survives an optimistic screen; the evidence does not establish that it will be ready in time.

I prioritize this next because its causal contribution is testable: add an internal signal, hold resources and useful performance comparable, and measure whether harmful outcomes decline. It might succeed without complete understanding or near-perfect runtime detection. This is a research-priority judgment, not a probability ranking of every safety approach.

From a proposal to interventions—with recurring obstacles

The theory of change: use model internals to expose failures or supply better training feedback; train a safer policy; retain the improvement in consequential deployment. A low internal loss alone does not demonstrate either behavioral safety or its persistence.

Larger models can help—and reveal a different gap

SOLiD uses internal lie detectors to improve preference labels. At training-detector recall of 99% and target KL divergence 0.15, its reported undetected deception falls from 34% at 1B to 14% at 405B. The largest model has one seed. This is a model-size comparison, not annual research progress.

The outcome combines behavior and detection: a lower value can mean fewer lies or more detectable lies. A separate spontaneous-lie analysis reports only 9% flagged at 8B and 2% at 70B at the paper’s decision boundary. These are different populations and denominators; they cannot be subtracted from the headline result.

Implication: increasing scale need not defeat internal supervision. But a favorable scaling curve on familiar data cannot establish coverage of strategically important new behavior. Measure relevant capability and fresh hazards alongside size.

The study also reports a lower-cost variant that rejects flagged training responses instead of escalating them, with comparable tested outcomes. It still needs detector training and validation. This is a concrete new implementation of the training route, rather than evidence that the original safety problem has disappeared. Protocol and results.

The probe checkpoints start from an abliterated model plus a merged safety adapter; original Instruct has a different training history. The comparison does not isolate the cause. Post-continuation utility is unreported. Full harmful-data mixtures and reported dispersion are in the ledger.

What this falsifies: treating these checkpoints’ initial robustness as evidence of durable safety through ordinary further training. What survives: a final intervention before deployment, or an effective repair and revalidation cycle. Neither requires immunity to arbitrary malicious weight changes.

A frozen deployment avoids subsequent weight updates, but still needs resilience to unfamiliar tasks and in-context adaptation. Reapplying a safety method could solve the continuation problem, provided it preserves useful learning and can be validated before exposure. Updating a probe during safety training is not evidence that this complete repair cycle works.

Measure retained safety benefit, then its pace

The metric: at fixed total defense cost and minimum useful task performance, measure the fraction of independently evaluated harmful episodes prevented by adding internal training signals to the best behavioral pipeline. Repeat after specified kinds and amounts of continued training, allowing the proposed repair process. Compare old and new methods on both frozen and fresh, more capable targets. Report absolute failure rates, uncertainty and the incremental reduction.

Incremental reduction = 1 − failure rate with internal training / failure rate with behavioral training alone

A proposed episode-level metric, not a conversion of the published scores above. It can be negative. When the baseline has zero observed failures, report uncertainty bounds instead of dividing by zero.

Track the largest tested continuation budget over which a protocol meets the chosen safety threshold. Keep benign learning, reward hacking and malicious weight changes separate. Include useful capability gained during that continuation: preventing learning altogether is not a satisfactory repair.

Current pace assessment: the selected record advances from proposals to working interventions and larger-model tests. Comparable problems recur as evaluation changes. It does not yet supply a rate of durable improvement. There is no defensible extrapolation from these heterogeneous milestones to a catastrophe-prevention date.

The next experiment should try to break the strongest repair

Train matched behavioral-only and internally assisted systems, including a control with equally strong regularization but no internal signal; continue useful capability training; reapply each safety method within equal total budgets; then test fresh harmful opportunities using independent evaluators. Optimize attacks separately against each system. Report useful task completion, harmful outcomes, false alarms, lost monitorability, compute and repair time. Repeat across target capability and training budgets.

  • Invest further if the additional benefit survives fresh hazard families and repeated repair cycles while preserving useful learning.
  • Deprioritize the tested training program if its strongest affordable repairs repeatedly lose the benefit—even with controlled model provenance and favorable deployment assumptions.
  • Do not infer that all interpretability is doomed from this route failing. Auditing, scientific discovery and consequential warnings have different requirements.

“Fast enough” requires a target deployment, a tolerated residual failure rate and a deadline range. The decision is whether the tested capability and training envelope covers that deployment in time. Those inputs cannot be recovered from publication dates or benchmark scores.
