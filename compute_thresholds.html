Do Compute Thresholds Still Catch Frontier-Level AI?

A large share of AI governance research on catastrophic risk follows one theory of change: regulate the models trained with the most compute. Training compute is measurable, known before deployment and checkable by outsiders, and it tracks capability. A threshold in FLOP can therefore pick out the few models that need evaluations and risk management, without burdening everyone else (Sastry et al., 2024; Heim & Koessler, 2024). The idea has spread: thresholds of 1025 or 1026 FLOP now appear in EU, US state and South Korean law. Getting the idea into law, however, is only one step of the theory. This page tests the step it rests on: that a model below the threshold can't do what frontier models can.

What the data says

  • Models below 1025 FLOP matched GPT-4 before the EU's obligations applied. The EU set its threshold at GPT-4's scale in December 2023. Llama 3.1 70B (about 7.9×1024 FLOP) matched GPT-4 in July 2024, and Qwen2.5 72B passed it in September 2024. The obligations only applied from August 2025. In 2026, models estimated at 2.5–4.3×1024 FLOP (DeepSeek V4 Flash, V4.1 Flash) score above GPT-5.
  • The frontier itself may sit below 1026. Epoch's estimate for GPT-5, the frontier when California signed SB 53, is about 6.6×1025 FLOP (speculative; range 2×1025 to 2×1026). If it is right, a law aimed at the frontier didn't clearly cover the frontier model of its own day.
  • The research saw this coming; policy hasn't followed. Heim & Koessler call compute thresholds a first filter that should be updated and complemented with other metrics, and Hooker (2024) argues the compute–risk link is too unstable to rely on. Yet every law since 2023 has reused 1025 or 1026. The EU can change its threshold by delegated act (Article 51(3)) and can designate models on capability alone, but the threshold is still 1025. The US state laws also require $500 million in annual revenue, which ties them to large developers rather than to capability.

How the comparison is built

Capability. Every model is placed with Epoch AI's Capabilities Index (ECI), a composite of many benchmarks. So that this page reads on the same scale as the AI control page, ECI is converted to a METR-style 50% time horizon with the same log-linear fit: ln(minutes) = -18.25 + 0.157 × ECI (R² = 0.97 across 21 models with both). The conversion is monotonic, so it changes no ordering or crossing date. Using one index for every model keeps frontier and below-threshold models on equal footing, since METR has measured few of the latter.

Judgement calls: (1) Compute. Training compute is Epoch AI's estimate from its AI models database. Most estimates for open models are 6 × active parameters × tokens and count pre-training only. Reinforcement learning after pre-training can add 10% to 100% or more (Epoch's own range for o3 and Grok 4), and the EU counts cumulative compute. The margin buttons handle this: at ×3 or ×10, a model counts as below only if its estimate is that many times under the threshold. The main result holds even at ×10 for the 1025 threshold. (2) Frontier. A frontier model is one with a higher ECI than every model released before it, from GPT-4 (March 2023) on. Models without an ECI score aren't placed. (3) What the threshold was "meant" to catch. The brown lines use the frontier's capability on the date each law was adopted or its threshold agreed (December 2023 for the EU AI Act). This is a proxy for intent; laws also aim to cover future, more capable models. (4) Months behind. For each week, the lower panel finds the most capable model below the threshold so far, and reports how long ago a frontier model first reached that capability. It starts once a model below the threshold matched GPT-4, the earliest frontier model in the data. (5) Capability is not risk. ECI measures general benchmark performance. Specific dangerous capabilities (bio, cyber, autonomy) may track compute differently, and a capable model can still be deployed safely. The page tests whether thresholds pick out capable models, not whether those models are dangerous.
