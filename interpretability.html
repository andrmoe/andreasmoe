Can internal monitors become decisive?

Read a model’s internal activations, detect a dangerous action, and stop it before it takes effect. This route may need a few reliable signals rather than a complete explanation of the model.

My screening judgment: investigate further. Published results demonstrate useful signals and potentially cheap readouts. They do not establish robust prevention. The optimistic scenario survives; the central uncertainty is whether errors persist across genuinely different opportunities to intervene.

This is an assessment of runtime activation monitoring, including probes and representation readouts. These are useful internal-access tools, but successful classification alone is not evidence of mechanistic understanding. A failure of this route would not rule out interpretability-guided training, editing, architecture design or verification.

The metric

Miss fraction = 1 − recall, at a stated false-positive rate

Measure the share of dangerous examples missed by an internal monitor at a disclosed false-alarm threshold, alongside monitoring cost and a behavioral baseline on the same examples. An optimistic scenario then asks how many new, informative checkpoints before harm could reduce the missed fraction to a chosen target. Keep the task, target model, negative examples and attacker access attached to every result.

“Decisive” is a decision threshold to explore here, not a benchmark label: for example, reducing the chance of an attempted harmful episode escaping intervention by 1,000× within an affordable monitoring budget. The available studies mostly measure classifications, not that episode-level outcome.

Chronology and inclusion decisions

An optimistic cost-to-coverage model

Scenario, not a fit or a forecast

Start with a measured single-check detection rate. Optimistically assume it transfers, flagged actions can be blocked, and a harmful episode supplies several informative checkpoints. A detection result on a completed tool call would need to become a pre-execution check to support this assumption.

Cost assumptions · editable, not measurements of the selected monitor

A reference pass is one full text-monitor evaluation of a comparable interaction. Defaults illustrate inexpensive checks; they are not a dollar-cost estimate. Alarm handling, follow-up inference and setup can dominate the small classifier head.

━ Selected persistence┄ Independent misses┄ Target escaped fractionGray line: cost budget

Repeated scoring of the same deterministic activations provides no extra chances. The independent curve requires new information and equal detection quality at each checkpoint. Fully persistent errors never improve. The model does not estimate catastrophic risk, the number of harmful behaviors in existence, or the combined failure of a safety stack.

Equations and what would falsify the optimistic assumptions
m = 1 − r·t   b = z·m
E(k) = b + (1 − b) [(m − b)/(1 − b)]k
C(k) = A/N + k(c + αh)

r is measured recall, t the assumed retained fraction, z the persistent share of misses, and k the effective checkpoints. The first check always has miss fraction m. The irreducible floor is b. For b = 1, E(k) = 1. A/N amortizes setup, c prices a check and αh prices expected false alarms on benign traffic. All k checks are charged; true-positive handling is omitted for an optimistic rare-event screen. The smallest feasible k defines this protocol’s modeled cost to reach the target, not a global minimum across possible methods.

Evidence that an attacker can reliably occupy the blind spot, that the first dangerous action is irreversible, or that the monitor sees its signal only after harm would invalidate the favorable scenario. A larger model producing more tokens does not automatically supply more useful checkpoints. A retained-recall factor is a sensitivity assumption, not a measured law of transfer.

With 73% single-check recall, independent misses give 0.276 ≈ 0.039% after six checks. If just 1% of the original misses are permanently invisible, the floor is 0.27%: a 0.1% target becomes unreachable even with unlimited checks. Neither dependence assumption is established by the single-check measurement.

Larger models: cost and detection can move differently

The FIBS study reports better ranking quality on larger Qwen models: the four probes span 0.585–0.781 AUROC on 2B, versus 0.963–0.986 across models from 27B upward. Those are ranges across methods/models, not confidence intervals or fixed-FPR recall. Parameter count is not a general capability score. §5.1.

An existing activation can be cheap to classify even when the model that produced it is large. The 2025 high-stakes paper explicitly assumes those activations are already available. Its cost model gives an attention-probe head 16,384·S operations, versus 1.6×1010·S + 262,144·S² for a separate 8B reference pass. Tables 5–6.

This is inference arithmetic under the paper’s accounting, not measured latency. It excludes activation transport, model instrumentation, probe training and human validation. Follow-up questions can require extra model computation; their cost must be included. The cheaper-head example is not a measurement of FIBS.

A cascade in the same high-stakes study matched a standalone 8B classifier’s aggregate AUROC while invoking it on 40% of cases. That is a useful cost precedent, not a demonstrated cost for 99.9% detection. Figure 6.

What deserves the next research dollar?

Measure errors across whole adversarial episodes, before irreversible actions. Compare an internal monitor, a strong behavioral monitor and their combination on the same trajectories. Record joint misses, false alarms, actor task performance and actual compute/latency. This would test the assumptions that dominate the optimistic calculation.

Obfuscation attacks can defeat strong-looking detectors; Neural Chameleons demonstrate transfer of an evasive skill to previously unseen safety probes. These are concrete reasons to test adaptive adversaries, rather than extrapolate ordinary classification accuracy. They do not prove that every possible internal monitor fails. Obfuscated Activations · Neural Chameleons.

My preference for this route is a prioritization judgment: it has a limited target, reusable internal signals, a plausible connection to interruption, and quantitative evidence. It does not yet have a demonstrated preventive guarantee. Favorable outcomes justify deeper investigation; failure under one attack model or scenario only rejects that configuration.

Trying to falsify catastrophe prevention

Adversarial review · 11 November 2026

The original safety case was broader than monitoring: systematic audits, feedback on internal processes, deliberate design, and extracting knowledge without deploying agents. Research has produced substantial scientific and technical progress, but useful internal signals do not establish the stronger ambition of detecting hidden danger that survives behavioral tests. Historical assessment: original justifications, progress, required thresholds, changing goals and new safety ideas.

The strongest challenge is marginal value against an adaptive adversary. An internal monitor might miss exactly the dangerous episodes that a strong behavioral monitor misses. An attacker may repeatedly select those blind spots. High average recall and cheap classification do not resolve either problem.

Incremental reduction = baseline episode escape rate / internally assisted episode escape rate

Compare stacks at equal total defense cost and useful task performance, optimizing attacks separately against each under the same declared attack budget. Report absolute escape rates and uncertainty alongside the ratio. A paired evaluation on shared trajectories reveals overlapping failures; separate attack searches test adaptation. Neither is supplied by the single-check scenario above.

Other routes need different falsification tests
  • Deployment audits: can an internal tool change a correct deployment decision beyond an equally resourced behavioral audit? Introspection adapters improve AuditBench investigator success from the previous best 53% to 59%, but a separate test surfaces symptoms without revealing the underlying reward-seeking objective. One credible symptom might still justify stopping deployment. Results and scoring protocol.
  • Training and editing: does safety persist under new tasks and further optimization, or does only the measured signal disappear? Frozen probes can be exploited, while continuously updated probes improve tested behavior and preserve detectability. This is evidence against declaring the training route doomed. Probe-guided fine-tuning.
  • Construction and verification: can guarantees scale at an affordable capability and proof cost? Existing sparse-model and small-transformer proof results leave a substantial gap; a gap is not an impossibility result. Narrow safety-critical components remain a possible use. Sparse transformers · Compact proofs.
  • Scientific discovery: does a finding enable a timely safety decision that comparable behavioral investigation would miss? A few consequential discoveries could matter without comprehensive coverage.

Coverage of the threat matters too. If an intervention reaches fraction q of baseline catastrophic risk and reduces that portion by factor R, simple accounting gives residual risk (1 − q) + q/R, assuming other risks stay fixed and no new harm is induced. Even perfect monitoring cannot eliminate risk outside its channel. Neither q nor R is estimated here.

Updated judgment: current evidence does not establish a decisive safety barrier. It also does not falsify a decisive future contribution. Prioritize experiments measuring additional prevention and consequential audit decisions. Failure of one monitoring configuration does not reject the other routes.
