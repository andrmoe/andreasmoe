Alignment through understood construction · Reviewed 12 November 2026

How capable is the strongest system we can align through understanding?

Track demonstrated systems whose construction supports an alignment argument, then ask whether their capabilities are catching up with the general AI frontier.

Current result: the general-purpose frontier is not established. This review identifies bounded construction certificates, partial explanations and conditional safety designs, but no implemented general-purpose system that qualifies. The missing observation is not a score of zero, and it does not establish that this route will fail.

The search covers 15 candidate studies spanning 2019–2026, including agent-level work on assistance games and corrigible goals. It is a purposive review, not an exhaustive inventory. The current evidence supports a ledger of achievements and gaps; it does not support a historical growth rate, a closing capability gap, or a probability of readiness by general superhuman AI.

Measure the capability of systems that satisfy the property

Proposed metric: track the most capable complete system whose alignment within its stated operating scope is substantiated by understanding its construction. Record independent capability evaluations, the alignment claim, the scope and strength of the evidence, and construction and verification costs. Compare capability only between systems with compatible evaluations and safety claims.

The destination: a useful general-purpose system whose internal organization gives us a justified reason to expect aligned choices when its abilities exceed human supervision. Neither explaining an isolated circuit nor making a weak system harmless establishes that property.

A qualifying general-purpose observation needs an implemented system, measured competence, an explicit alignment requirement and operating scope, an auditable construction-based argument, and evidence that the actual implementation satisfies that argument. The treatment of unexplained components, tool use and any permitted learning must be explicit. A formal proof is one possible form of evidence, not a mandatory research method.

Scope matters: bounded task guarantees remain valid achievements. They are recorded below, but are not silently relabeled as general alignment. A capable model with a few understood circuits does not inherit a whole-system safety claim. A conditional theorem does not inherit the capabilities of an implementation that has not been demonstrated.

There is no restriction to neural networks or human-readable descriptions of every weight. Compact invariants can count as understanding. The decision must be justified for the complete system in scope. An external enforcement mechanism belongs in the control assessment unless the proposed construction also establishes alignment of the system itself.

The strongest near-matches establish different things

The historical baseline includes actual agent-incentive construction: Holtman's 2020 utility-update design combines conditional proofs with toy-world policy simulations. It also examines incomplete incentive suppression and the gap between the mathematical model and physical implementation. Its bounded success should not be erased by asking only about current language models.

Agent-level evidence deserves particular attention. AssistanceZero implements an assistance-game approach in Minecraft. Human participants rated its helpfulness at 3.1 ± 0.4 / 5, versus 1.7 ± 0.3 for SFT and 4.0 ± 0.5 for an expert human assistant (reported 90% confidence intervals). This measures useful cooperation, not general alignment. The construction still relies on approximate planning and learned models of human behavior.

A corrigibility transformation supplies conditional incentive results and gridworld experiments. In its later LLM prompt study, monitor disabling falls from 73.4% to 1.0%, averaged across three models. Reward hacking remains 92.2% under the corrigibility prompt: the intended goal is still to pass impossible tests while accepting monitoring. This separates a valuable safety property from complete alignment, and prompting a goal does not establish that the model's internal objective satisfies the theorem.

What does this tell us about readiness in time?

It does not yet establish a pace. Stability certificates, accuracy proofs, circuit explanations and Predictor safety theorems cover different properties. Connecting them into a single rising line would manufacture comparability. A blank general-purpose frontier is also not evidence that the underlying research is stationary.

The optimistic screen remains open: bounded certificates and interpretable construction supply concrete footholds. But the reviewed evidence gives no empirical basis for extrapolating to aligned general superhuman systems. A numerical readiness forecast would presently depend mainly on judgments about unobserved breakthroughs and AI timelines.

The next qualifying observation: a useful agent with a stated operating scope, meaningful opportunities to violate its intended requirements, and a construction-based argument explaining why it will satisfy them. The actual implementation must be checked, including the influence of components outside the explanation. Report task performance and independent attempts to invalidate the argument. Limited scope is acceptable if it stays explicit; a narrow certificate is not the endpoint.

Once comparable systems exist, test newer construction methods on the same capability and safety tasks, and test whether they extend to stronger systems at feasible cost. Compare their competence with contemporary unconstrained systems using the same evaluations. Keep scope expansions visible: a score increase obtained by narrowing the alignment claim is not progress on this metric.

For the deadline, assess capability development and construction progress together. More capable AI may accelerate research and also shorten the time available. Do not assume a fixed time lag or award credit for an unspecified future AI researcher. A system that only enables later alignment research still needs that remaining work assessed.
